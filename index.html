02 / Spot the warning signs

Practice with fictional messages.

These examples are invented for education. Never paste real conversations or personal information into a website to get a safety verdict.

“Hey, don't tell anyone we're chatting. Send me a photo so I know I can trust you.”

Secrecy requests and pressure to share photos can be warning signs. You can stop responding, block the account, and talk to a trusted adult or support person. A single message does not establish someone's identity or intent.

“Your account is locked. Click this link and enter your password immediately.”

Urgent requests for passwords through links can be phishing. Open the service through its official app or a bookmark instead. Do not enter a password through an unexpected message link.

03 / Support and boundaries

You deserve control over your digital space.

If you suspect someone monitors your device or accounts, changing settings may alert them. Consider using a device they cannot access and getting guidance from a trusted local support organization.

This site is educational, not emergency response, legal advice, or a substitute for a qualified safety professional. If you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services when safe to do so.
